Foreigners cannot own freehold (Hak Milik) land in Indonesia. However, they can legally control and profit from property through three structures: the Right to Use title (Hak Pakai) for a personal residence, a leasehold agreement (Hak Sewa), or a foreign-owned company (PT PMA) that holds the Right to Build (Hak Guna Bangunan). Each is legal and each has trade-offs.

The confusion around this single question costs foreign investors more money than almost anything else in the Indonesian market. Below is exactly how ownership works, which structure fits which goal, and the one arrangement you should never use.

## Can a foreigner own land in Indonesia outright?

No. Freehold ownership (Hak Milik) is reserved for Indonesian citizens only. This is written into the 1960 Basic Agrarian Law and has not changed. Any marketing that promises a foreigner "freehold ownership" is either misleading or describing an illegal nominee structure. What foreigners can legally hold are use rights and lease rights, which in practice deliver most of the economic benefit of ownership.

Think of it less as "can I own it" and more as "which legal right do I hold, for how long, and what can I do with it." Once you reframe it that way, Indonesia becomes far more navigable.

## What are the legal ways foreigners can hold property?

There are three structures worth knowing. Here is how they compare:

| Structure | What it is | Typical term | Best for |
|---|---|---|---|
| Hak Pakai (Right to Use) | A use title registered to a foreigner with residency | 30 years, extendable +20, then renewable +30 | A personal home or villa to live in |
| Hak Sewa (Leasehold) | A private lease contract with the landowner | Commonly 25–30 years, negotiable | Lower upfront cost, flexibility |
| PT PMA + HGB (Right to Build) | A foreign-owned company holding the build title | 30 years, extendable +20, then +30 | Rental income and business use |

### Hak Pakai (Right to Use)

Hak Pakai lets a foreigner with a residency permit (KITAS or KITAP) hold a registered right to use a property as a residence. It is the closest thing to ownership available to an individual foreigner. The term is 30 years, extendable by 20, and renewable for another 30 — a horizon long enough for most personal plans. Some regions set a minimum purchase price for Hak Pakai properties, which keeps it oriented toward mid- and upper-market homes.

### Hak Sewa (Leasehold)

A leasehold is simply a long-term rental contract registered with a notary. You pay for the right to use the land and any buildings for a fixed number of years. Leasehold has the lowest barrier to entry, does not require residency, and is common in Bali for villas. The trade-off is that the asset reverts to the landowner at the end of the term unless you negotiate an extension up front.

### PT PMA (foreign-owned company)

If your goal is rental income or running a business, a PT PMA is usually the cleanest path. It is a foreign investment limited liability company that can hold the Right to Build (Hak Guna Bangunan), legally rent out property, and repatriate profits. It carries setup and annual compliance costs, so it makes sense above a certain investment size rather than for a single small purchase.

## What is the nominee structure, and why should you avoid it?

A nominee arrangement is where a foreigner buys freehold land in an Indonesian citizen's name and signs side agreements to "control" it. Do not do this. Nominee structures are not recognized by Indonesian courts, which means the side agreements are legally unenforceable. If the nominee sells the land, dies, divorces, or simply refuses to cooperate, you can lose the entire investment with no legal recourse.

Every horror story you have heard about foreigners losing property in Bali traces back to this structure. The legal options above exist precisely so you never have to take that risk.

## How do I choose the right structure?

Match the structure to your actual goal:

1. **Buying a home to live in** → Hak Pakai if you have residency, or leasehold if you do not.
2. **Lower budget or short horizon** → Leasehold for flexibility and a smaller upfront commitment.
3. **Earning rental income or building a portfolio** → PT PMA with HGB so the rental activity is fully legal and profits can be repatriated.

The right answer also depends on your tax residency, how long you plan to hold, and whether you want to rent the property out. Get a qualified Indonesian notary (PPAT) and a tax advisor involved before you transfer any money.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Do I need to live in Indonesia to buy property?

Not for leasehold, which requires no residency permit. Hak Pakai for a residence requires a KITAS or KITAP residency permit. A PT PMA does not require you to personally hold residency, though directors may need work permits depending on the setup.

### How long can a foreigner hold property in Indonesia?

Through Hak Pakai or HGB, the practical horizon is 30 years extendable by 20 and renewable for another 30 — up to roughly 80 years in total. Leasehold terms are negotiated directly and commonly run 25–30 years with extension options.

### Is buying property in Indonesia safe for foreigners?

Yes, when you use a legal structure, verify the land title, and work with a licensed notary. The risk comes almost entirely from nominee arrangements and skipping due diligence, both of which are avoidable.

### Can I rent out a property I hold under Hak Pakai?

Hak Pakai is intended for residential use, not commercial rental. If your primary goal is rental income, a PT PMA is the appropriate structure to keep the activity legal.
