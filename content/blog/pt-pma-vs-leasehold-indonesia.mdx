Use a PT PMA when your goal is rental income, multiple properties, or running a business, because it makes commercial activity legal and lets you repatriate profits. Use a leasehold when you want lower upfront cost, no company overhead, and primarily personal use. The decision comes down to whether you are investing for income or buying for lifestyle.

Both are legal and widely used. The mistake is choosing based on cost alone rather than on what you actually plan to do with the property.

## What is a PT PMA?

A PT PMA (Perseroan Terbatas Penanaman Modal Asing) is a foreign-owned limited liability company registered in Indonesia. It can hold the Right to Build (Hak Guna Bangunan), legally operate rental and hospitality businesses, employ staff, and repatriate profits abroad. For investors who want to earn income from property, it is the structure that keeps everything above board.

A PT PMA has real obligations: a minimum investment plan, paid-up capital requirements, monthly and annual tax filings, and ongoing corporate compliance. These costs are why it suits larger or income-producing investments rather than a single small purchase.

## What is a leasehold?

A leasehold (Hak Sewa) is a private contract giving you the right to use land and buildings for a fixed term, commonly 25–30 years in Bali. It is registered with a notary, requires no company, and needs no residency permit. Leasehold is the simplest and cheapest way for a foreigner to control property, which is why it dominates the villa market.

The trade-off is finite duration and limited commercial flexibility. At the end of the term, the property reverts to the landowner unless you secured an extension up front.

## PT PMA vs leasehold: how do they compare?

| Factor | PT PMA + HGB | Leasehold (Hak Sewa) |
|---|---|---|
| Upfront cost | Higher (setup + capital) | Lower |
| Ongoing cost | Monthly/annual compliance | Minimal |
| Term | 30 yrs +20 +30 | Usually 25–30 yrs, negotiable |
| Rental income | Fully legal | Legally limited |
| Residency needed | No (directors may need permits) | No |
| Profit repatriation | Yes | N/A |
| Best for | Income, portfolios, business | Personal use, lower budget |

## When does a PT PMA make sense?

Choose a PT PMA if any of these apply:

1. You plan to **rent the property out** as your main objective.
2. You intend to acquire **multiple properties** over time.
3. You want to run a **hospitality or other business** alongside the property.
4. You need to **repatriate rental profits** to your home country cleanly.
5. The investment is **large enough** to absorb setup and annual compliance costs.

## When is leasehold the better choice?

Choose leasehold if:

1. The property is mainly for **personal or family use**.
2. You want the **lowest possible entry cost** and no company overhead.
3. Your **time horizon** fits comfortably within the lease term.
4. You value **simplicity** over commercial flexibility.

A common path is to start with a leasehold for a personal villa, then form a PT PMA later if you decide to build an income portfolio. The two are not mutually exclusive over the life of your investing.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### How much does it cost to set up a PT PMA?

Setup costs vary by provider and structure but typically run a few thousand US dollars, plus paid-up capital requirements and recurring annual compliance and tax filing fees. Get a fixed quote from a reputable corporate services firm before committing.

### Can I convert a leasehold into a PT PMA-held property later?

Not directly — they are different legal rights. You would acquire a new right (HGB) through the PT PMA. If you anticipate going commercial, it is cheaper to plan the structure correctly from the start.

### Does a PT PMA let me get residency?

A PT PMA can sponsor work and stay permits for directors and employees under certain conditions, which is one reason investors use it beyond just property. Requirements change, so confirm the current rules with an immigration specialist.
